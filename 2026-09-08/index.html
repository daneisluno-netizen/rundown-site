Chris Williamson (Modern Wisdom) — Dr Sasha Hamdani on Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria

VERDICT: Watch it.

1h24m video → ~10m read | Modern Wisdom | uploaded 7 Sep 2026

Topics covered
  • Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria — coined by psychiatrist Dr William Dodson (confirmed independently), framed by Hamdani as the far end of an emotional-sensitivity spectrum rather than a standalone diagnosis.
  • Neuroscience — a hyperactive amygdala, an underactive prefrontal cortex, and a frayed link between the two, compounded by dopamine and norepinephrine dysregulation, is her explanation for the physical "can't breathe" panic response to criticism or rejection.
  • Hormones — falling oestrogen lowers dopamine and worsens emotional regulation for roughly two weeks of every cycle; falling testosterone in men's 50s–60s can trigger a late-life surge in emotional sensitivity men aren't equipped to handle.
  • ! ADHD link — she states almost all adults with ADHD show some degree of RSD. Independent sources put comorbidity at 98–99%, but RSD is not a DSM-5 diagnosis — see Verification.
  • Men and anger — boys are steered toward anger as the only socially acceptable emotion; she ties this directly to both avoidant men and the UK's male suicide toll with a specific statistic — see Verification, likely overstated.
  • Calm Your Mind toolkit — her top three regulation tools: movement to interrupt the amygdala loop, deliberately delaying your reaction, and non-judgemental noticing (explicitly not "empty your mind") mindfulness.
  • Parenting — the "heel of bread" reframe: validate the feeling behind a child's (or adult's) disproportionate reaction without validating that the catastrophe is real or unsolvable.
  • ! Postpartum brain — cites roughly 2.5% grey matter loss in new mothers; the study she's drawing on found closer to 4.9% — see Verification, understated by about half.

WORTH YOUR TIME IF: you or someone close has ADHD, RSD, or a pattern of pre-emptively ending relationships or avoiding opportunities around a fear of rejection. SKIP IF: you want validated clinical protocols rather than a psychiatrist's personal synthesis and anecdotes — this is a construct, not a settled diagnosis.

Bottom line

Hamdani gives the clearest lay explanation of RSD's actual neuroscience (amygdala/PFC/dopamine/norepinephrine) I've seen briefed, and the three-tool "Calm Your Mind" kit is genuinely usable without buying her book. The two headline statistics she drops — near-universal ADHD comorbidity and a UK male-suicide figure — don't hold up equally well against the record: the ADHD figure is roughly right, the suicide figure looks overstated against the closest national data available.

Dan Martell — The GAIN Matrix for deciding what to hand to AI

VERDICT: Watch it (the framework), skip the self-promotion.

14m51s video → ~4m read | Dan Martell | uploaded 11 Jun 2026 (requeued twice, cleared gate today)

Topics covered
  • The GAIN matrix — four quadrants (Give, Accelerate, Integrate, No AI) sorted on two axes: easy/hard for humans, easy/hard for computers. A genuinely reusable filter for "should this task go to AI", not another prompting tip.
  • Give quadrant — fully-automatable tier: meeting notes (he names Granola), dashboards/report-pulling, and message triage. Gated by his rule of 3 R's: is it repetitive, is it rules-based, does it generate a return.
  • Accelerate quadrant — research, data analysis, and market scanning, where AI does the heavy lifting a human would otherwise need a specialist for. His 10/80/10 rule: human supplies 10% direction, AI does 80% of the work, human does the final 10% polish.
  • Integrate quadrant — creative and strategic work (workbook design, app prototypes) where AI executes but a human still has to supply "taste, vision, care" — his example is building an AI-generated workbook from his own book, then handing the output to a human designer.
  • No AI quadrant — leadership, persuasion/negotiation, coaching, and networking are flagged as never-outsource, on the argument that people follow and trust people, not computers.
  • ! Unverified reference — cites a "GDP valve" tool/study supposedly showing where AI is displacing jobs by percentage. No such named tool was found — likely an auto-caption mangling of something else entirely; treat as unconfirmed rather than a real source.

WORTH YOUR TIME IF: you want one applied filter for deciding what to delegate to AI versus keep for yourself, and can filter out the pitch. SKIP IF: you're not in the mood for the wrapped-in self-promotion (free AI-stack DM offer, Apex waitlist, book plug) — none of which is optional viewing.

Bottom line

The GAIN matrix itself is a clean, applicable framework and the rule of 3 R's is a genuinely useful one-line gate before automating anything. But nothing in the video is backed by shown data or a case study — it's all asserted from Martell's own results ("built companies past $1M in under 6 months"), and the one external reference he makes ("GDP valve") doesn't check out under that name.
