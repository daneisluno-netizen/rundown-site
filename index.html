Your SSPR Is About To Stop Working — Jonathan Edwards

WATCH IT.

Named dated change, a control you can audit in your tenants today, with a five-minute Entra demo showing exactly where to look.

Topics covered
  • SSPR registered-methods-only — a real, dated Entra change, but the exact date is contested even in Microsoft's own docs. ! Video says enforcement is 9 Nov 2026; the live Microsoft Learn page and a third-party MC mirror give three different day numbers for the same change (see Verification).
  • Root cause — "name tag" vs "registered" method. A phone number synced into mobilePhone/businessPhone/otherMails from on-prem AD (or typed in once) looks identical to a verified authentication method in the portal, but SSPR has been accepting both. After enforcement it accepts only the verified one.
  • 86% of SSPR verifications already use registered methods — confirmed by Microsoft's own Message Center post; the other 14% is your at-risk population.
  • Pairs with the SMS/voice MFA retirement (1 Feb 2027) — fixing an unregistered phone number by re-registering the same phone number just re-solves for a method that's also being retired in three months. Register a passkey instead to fix both in one pass.
  • Demo: Entra admin center → Identity → Authentication methods → User registration details → filter by "SSPR capable" and by method type (e.g. passkey) to find who's exposed, tenant by tenant.
  • Registration campaign nudges start 5 Oct 2026 per Microsoft — the one date every source agrees on.
Worth your time if / skip if

Worth your time if: you run MSP tenants and want the exact admin-center path to find at-risk users before the deadline, whichever day it lands on. Skip if: you already have a registration-campaign policy live and passkey-only rollout in progress — the demo tells you nothing new.

Bottom line

A real Entra Secure Future Initiative change is landing on your tenants in October/November 2026: SSPR will stop trusting an unregistered phone number sitting on a profile and will only accept methods a user has actually proven they control. The mechanism and the audit path are solid and worth five minutes. The specific enforcement date in the video should not be trusted without a fresh check closer to the time — see below.

The Biggest Nutrition Scam "Experts" Are Pushing — Chris Williamson w/ Daniel Lieberman

WATCH IT

— 10 minutes, well-evidenced debunk of a specific claimed pattern (Blue Zones), not a vague hot take.

Topics covered
  • Blue Zones "live to 100" claim rests on a tiny, noisy base rate — supposedly ~6 centenarians per 10,000 people vs ~3 per 10,000 in the US generally; a small absolute difference reads as a huge relative one.
  • Only 18% of validated supercentenarians worldwide have a birth certificate (per Saul Newman's research) — but Lieberman applies this figure specifically to "these blue zones," which overstates what the underlying study measured (see Verification).
  • Loma Linda, California is a Blue Zone because of religious lifestyle choices (Seventh-day Adventists — no smoking, exercise, often vegetarian), not a diet secret — undermines the idea that a shared diet explains all five zones.
  • "Optimal" is presented as a marketing word, not a scientific one — Lieberman's stated trigger phrase, tied to the argument that individual variation (age, genetics, goals) makes a universal "optimal diet" close to meaningless.
  • Low-carb vs low-fat: efficacy ≠ effectiveness — low-carb diets show slightly higher weight-loss efficacy in controlled trials, but the effectiveness gap narrows in the real world because low-carb is harder to sustain.
Worth your time if / skip if

Worth your time if: you want a specific, named mechanism for why the Blue Zones story is weaker than it's marketed as — useful ammunition next time it comes up. Skip if: you've already read Saul Newman's paper or the CNBC/UCL coverage of his Ig Nobel win — this covers the same ground in conversation form.

Bottom line

The Blue Zones claim doesn't hold up well under Lieberman's specific critique — tiny base rates, a confirmed record-keeping/fraud problem in the source data, and at least one "zone" (Loma Linda) explained by religious lifestyle rather than diet. The one place the clip overreaches is applying an 18%-birth-certificate statistic about supercentenarians generally to the Blue Zones specifically, which is a real but smaller claim than stated.
