This AI System Will Make You So Smart It's Almost Unfair — Dan Martell

Watch it — but you've already built most of this.

This describes, almost feature-for-feature, the Obsidian vault + identity-file + nightly-consolidation setup you already run. Worth 12 minutes to see where his version diverges from yours.

Dan Martell | 12m video → 3m read | 25 Jun 2026 | self lane

Topics covered
  • Three identity files — user.md (who you are, how you communicate), soul.md (how the AI should act — tone, values), identity.md (who the AI is, named). This is the same three-way split as your CLAUDE.md's own persona framing, just as three separate files instead of one.
  • Seven vault folders: people, projects, decisions, companies, meetings, daily, knowledge — plus an eighth "MOC" (map of content) folder for indexes once a topic gets messy. Your memory system already covers people/user/feedback/project but has no explicit "decisions" or "companies" folder.
  • Nightly automated consolidation via a scheduled Claude task — a cron-style job that reads everything added that day, finds orphan notes, merges duplicates, updates MOCs, flags anything strategic. Confirmed real: Claude Cowork now ships scheduled recurring tasks that do exactly this class of job.
  • The exact prompt he uses for meeting extraction (via Granola, a transcription tool): decisions/commitments/preferences/key-insights, output as markdown, skip small talk. Directly reusable regardless of which transcription tool feeds it.
  • ! Claims 60% → 85% "accuracy" improvement from folder structure alone — no methodology given, self-reported, not independently checkable. Treat as an anecdote, not a benchmark.
  • "Don't write the identity files yourself — have the AI interview you and draft them" — a genuinely different approach from writing CLAUDE.md by hand; worth trying on a section you haven't touched in a while, to see what it surfaces that you wouldn't have written yourself.

Microsoft Data Loss Prevention Full Course — T-Minus365

WATCH ONE SPECIFIC STRETCH.

100 minutes, but the useful third is 00:00-13:00 (framework + licensing) and 43:00-52:00 (protection-pack templates + business discovery). The middle is a click-through demo you can skip once you've built one policy yourself.

T-Minus365 (Nick, Microsoft MVP) | 1h40m video → 7m read | 6 Sep 2026 | work lane

Topics covered
  • Licensing has quietly split in two since May 2026 — Business Premium is no longer eligible for the enterprise Purview Suite add-on; Microsoft now sells a separate "Purview Suite for Business Premium" SKU (~£8-10/user/mo) capped at 300 users per tenant. Nick's advice ("bolt Purview Suite onto Business Premium") is still directionally correct, but he doesn't name the cap — a client over 300 seats on Business Premium needs E3/E5, not the add-on.
  • DLP policy = location + trigger + action, same mental model as Conditional Access. Sensitive Info Types (pattern/regex, included from Business Premium), trainable classifiers and Exact Data Match (both need Purview Suite add-on or E5) are the three classifier types, in increasing cost and decreasing false-positive rate.
  • Endpoint DLP needs devices onboarded to Defender for Endpoint or Business first — no separate onboarding step if they're already in Defender. Confirmed against Learn: also needs Purview Suite add-on or E5, Global/Security/Compliance admin to onboard, 24-48h before telemetry appears.
  • "Block access to external domains/users" for SharePoint/OneDrive is a real, dated feature — public preview since April 2026, GA rolling out May-July 2026. So as of this video it's brand new, not old guidance.
  • Copilot DLP can only restrict one of two things per rule: block Copilot from processing a sensitive prompt entirely, or block it from using that prompt for web search — confirmed on Learn as genuinely separate, mutually exclusive actions, not a demo limitation.
  • The three failure modes he names for DLP rollouts: alert fatigue (100+/day is typical on day one), exception creep (one exec gets blocked, "just turn it off"), and rule complexity nobody can audit later. All three are things you'd check for in an audit, not just avoid on a fresh build.
  • "Protection pack" naming convention and a business-outcome matrix — sell "block credit cards to Gmail" to a client, not "DLP policy for SIT 335". He has ~9 separate policies just for the finance pack (blanket block + separate warn-with-override for the accounting group).
  • ! Advanced hunting query in Defender (KQL) to aggregate alerts by sender/external domain — turns "100 alerts to review one by one" into one table. Requires E5 for Defender portal correlation; without it you're stuck in the slower Purview admin centre view he shows taking ~1 minute per page load.
Worth your time if / skip if

Worth your time if: you're about to scope or price a DLP engagement and want the licensing tiers and a template structure rather than starting from Microsoft's raw docs. Skip if: you've already built a Purview DLP policy before — the 00:17-00:43 and 00:54-01:26 stretches are pure click-through with nothing new in them.

Bottom line

A solid, current field guide to a Purview area most MSPs under-deliver on, built by someone who does the rollouts for a living — the "protection pack" framing and business-outcome matrix are the actual differentiators over Microsoft's own docs. The one gap that matters: he never mentions the May 2026 Business Premium licensing split, so anyone following this video's licensing advice for a >300-seat Business Premium client will hit a wall Microsoft put up months before this was filmed.
